# The Paid tier is a channel's subscription on Stripe's hosted pages, sold through Managed Payments

This answers the decision ADR 0015 left for later, payment for features (creator-analytics#5). ADR 0015 stays as written, and a Tip stays what it describes.

The Paid tier is a subscription that belongs to a channel, not to a user, because the Entitlement is the channel's (ADR 0001). Only the channel's owner, the user whose Membership has the `owner` role, may start a Checkout or open the Customer Portal for that channel. Agency billing for many channels (#7) is left open, and a per-channel Entitlement leaves room for it.

The price is $7 a month or $70 a year: one Stripe product with two prices. There is no trial. A free tier channel sees the paid screens with clearly marked sample data instead, so a creator sees what the Paid tier shows before paying for it.

A creator subscribes on Stripe's hosted Checkout, in subscription mode, and manages the subscription on Stripe's hosted Customer Portal: the card, the switch between monthly and annual, and cancellation at the end of the paid period. The API opens each one by a server redirect, so each is a navigation, as a Tip's Payment Link is. No Stripe script, no Stripe library and no Stripe host reaches a Stillstats page, and the content security policy stays as it is (ADR 0007, ADR 0014). Embedded checkout stays rejected, for the reason ADR 0015 gives.

The Paid tier is sold through Stripe Managed Payments, so Stripe is the merchant of record and collects and files the sales tax and VAT. A seller outside the EU and the UK owes their VAT from the first sale to a consumer, and monthly billing can trip the US states' transaction-count thresholds, so selling on my own account would mean tax registrations from the first subscriber. If Stripe declines the Managed Payments eligibility review, the same integration runs on plain Stripe with Stripe Tax, and Checkout accepts US billing addresses only until I register for VAT. Tips stay on Payment Links and off Managed Payments.

A failed payment changes nothing until Stripe's retries end the subscription.

A revocation, a disconnect or an account deletion cancels the subscription at once and refunds the unused part of the period. The history the subscription pays for is deleted within ADR 0006's windows, so the creator is not charged for days they can no longer use. This ADR states the rule; how Stripe carries out the refund under a merchant of record is checked when it is built (#784).

A later price rise is a new price for new subscribers. An existing subscription keeps its price while it stays active, and is never moved to the new one.

2026-11-18: the webhook reads a Stillstats subscription's plan from its item's billing interval, `price.recurring.interval` (`month` is monthly, `year` is annual), and no longer from a match of its price ID against `STRIPE_PRICE_MONTHLY` or `STRIPE_PRICE_ANNUAL` (creator-analytics#813). The channel ID in the subscription's metadata still marks it as Stillstats', since only Stillstats Checkout writes it, and a paid invoice counts by that metadata alone. The two settings still choose the prices Checkout sells. So a subscription that keeps its price after a price rise keeps its renewals, plan switches, past due status, deletion and payments recorded, and a subscription that bills at any other interval still records its events, its status, the end of its paid period and its purge date included, keeps the plan the row holds for the same subscription (a new or replacing one records no plan until its Checkout lands), and reports to GlitchTip unless its channel no longer exists, and its paid invoices count. The price match came from sharing the LLC's Stripe account with other products; Stillstats now has its own Stripe account, which holds only Stillstats products. A setting that lists retired price IDs was rejected: it needs a manual step at every price change, and a missed step drops events with no error.

The subscription ending at the end of its paid period, not the press of "Cancel", starts ADR 0006's 30-day window, and the channel's purge date is 30 days after that end. Until the purge date the history stays readable and no new reports are downloaded. A new subscription before it resumes the history and fills the gap from the files YouTube still holds.

2026-11-19: the Paid tier is now called Pro, and sold as "Stillstats Pro" (creator-analytics#913). The price is $7 a month or $70 a year for each channel, as above, which reverses the $4.95 and $49 of creator-analytics#908. The upgrade screen shows the amounts of the two configured Stripe prices, which the API reads from Stripe, so the screen never disagrees with Checkout. Code identifiers, database values, API fields, the `paid` Entitlement and analytics event names keep the word `paid`.

2026-11-19: a free tier channel no longer sees the paid screens with sample data (creator-analytics#915). The sample impressions and CTR read as the creator's own numbers, and I do not want the sales pitch in front of them, so nothing takes their place. There is still no trial. The way to Pro is the upgrade screen, the sidebar's "Upgrade" item, the homepage's "Subscribe" and Settings.
